Programme 01

The Musahar Community Scholarship Program

50meritorious students selected each year
6districts of Uttar Pradesh
₹15 lakhallocated annually
8th & 10thcompetitive scholarship examination

To promote educational equity and bring the highly marginalized Vanvasi Community which are commonly known in the region as Musahar into the mainstream of society, Tathagat Trust runs an annual merit-based scholarship program designed to encourage academic excellence and reduce school dropout rates among deserving students.

Every year, the Trust conducts a competitive scholarship examination for Musahar students who have successfully passed Class 8th and Class 10th. Based on their performance, 50 meritorious students are selected from six districts of Uttar Pradesh—Varanasi, Jaunpur, Chandauli, Azamgarh, Ghazipur, and Bhadohi. The scholarships provide financial assistance to enable students to continue their education without interruption and inspire them to pursue higher studies and meaningful careers.

In recognition of India's rich legacy of social justice and nation-building, the scholarships are named after contributors to nation-building from tribal communities, social reformers, freedom fighters, and champions of tribal and marginalized communities, so that students develop a sense of self-pride and national integration.

The scholarship program is more than financial support—it serves as a platform to identify and nurture talented youth from one of India's most disadvantaged communities, empowering them through education and creating pathways toward socio-economic upliftment.

Tathagat Trust allocates approximately ₹15 lakh annually to this initiative, reaffirming its long-term commitment to expanding educational opportunities and transforming the lives of Musahar children through the power of education.

Programme 02

Tathagat Industrial Training Institute (ITI) – Sonbhadra–Mirzapur Border, Uttar Pradesh

Established in 2011, the Tathagat Industrial Training Institute (ITI) was founded to empower the Kol & Khairwar community, one of the most marginalized tribal communities in the Sonbhadra–Mirzapur region of Uttar Pradesh. Recognizing that skill development is a powerful pathway to sustainable livelihoods and social mobility, the Institute was established to provide quality technical education to tribal youth who otherwise have limited access to vocational training and employment opportunities.

The Institute offers completely free, two-year technical and vocational training to students from economically disadvantaged tribal families, equipping them with industry-relevant skills, practical knowledge, and professional discipline. With a training capacity of 168 students each year, the Institute prepares young people for meaningful careers across various industrial and technical sectors.

Since its inception, the Tathagat ITI has trained more than 2,000 students, many of whom have secured employment in leading corporate organizations across India, significantly improving the socio-economic conditions of their families and communities.

A key pillar of this success has been the continued support of Maruti Suzuki, which has partnered with the Institute for over 11 years, providing sustained sponsorship and helping strengthen industry-linked training and placement opportunities. This long-standing collaboration has enabled hundreds of tribal youth to transition from marginalized backgrounds to stable, dignified employment, making the Institute a model for skill-based rural and tribal empowerment.

Programme 03

Bawaria Community Social Rehabilitation (Shamli District, Uttar Pradesh)

The Bawaria Community Social Rehabilitation Initiative was launched after 2013 when Dr. N.P. Singh, while serving as the District Magistrate of Shamli, Uttar Pradesh, conducted an in-depth assessment of the socio-economic conditions of the Bawaria community. Denotified and nomadic communities, particularly the Bawaria community across Haryana and Uttar Pradesh, were officially denotified by the Government of India in 1952 after having been unjustly categorized as "criminal tribes" under the British colonial regime. Despite this denotification after Independence, the community continued to face deep-rooted social stigma, economic exclusion, and limited access to education and livelihood opportunities.

Recognizing these persistent challenges, Dr. Singh initiated this rehabilitation initiative to restore dignity, expand educational opportunities, and support the socio-economic empowerment of the community.

Track I

Education & Mainstreaming

The Trust identifies talented students from the community and supports them in pursuing higher education, enabling them to break the cycle of poverty and social discrimination. With the motivation, mentorship, and educational support provided by the Trust, many young women from the Bawaria community have successfully pursued higher education at the University of Delhi and other reputed universities across the country. Several of them have also secured meaningful employment opportunities, demonstrating the transformative impact of education and serving as an inspiration for others in their community. Similar support has enabled dozens of young men and women from the Bawaria community to build successful careers as police officers, nurses, teachers, lawyers, senior corporate executives, and bank officers, becoming role models for future generations.

Track II

Livelihood Transformation

The Trust has also worked extensively to create sustainable livelihood opportunities for the community, particularly for women. Women who were once dependent on illegal liquor brewing were encouraged and supported to dismantle illicit distilleries and transition to dignified, lawful means of earning a livelihood. Through the formation of grassroots Self-Help Groups (SHGs), including the Ekta Samooh, women received support in micro-savings, financial literacy, and entrepreneurship. The Trust facilitated the provision of leaf-plate-making machines and other income-generating resources, enabling them to establish legal micro-enterprises and achieve greater economic independence.

Through education, livelihood generation, women's empowerment, and social inclusion, the initiative has helped transform lives, restore dignity, and create sustainable opportunities for one of India's most marginalized nomadic communities.

Community Focus

Chhattisgarh — Naxal-Affected Regions & Tribal Community Development

The Trust is actively working in the Naxal-affected regions of Chhattisgarh, engaging youth from the Gond, Halba, Satnami, and other tribal and underprivileged communities through education, skill development, sports, and livelihood-oriented initiatives.

  • Gond
  • Halba
  • Satnami

The Satnami community, one of central India's most prominent communities with a rich legacy of social reform and resistance, has historically endured prolonged socio-economic deprivation. The community's history includes its courageous struggle against oppression, including its resistance during the reign of Aurangzeb. Recognizing the continuing need for educational advancement and social empowerment, the Trust supports the Satnami community through quality education, skill development, youth empowerment, and inclusive community development programmes.

Bheja Jangali Model School (Kanker/Balod, Chhattisgarh)

Situated in Bastar—the gateway to the Naxal-affected region—the Bheja Jangali Model School will provide free, high-quality education from Bal Vatika to Class 12, along with vocational skills, sports training, and career guidance for children and youth belonging primarily to the Halba, Gond, Satnami, and other neighbouring tribal communities.

Adjacent to the school, the Youth Sports Development & Fitness Complex, being developed in collaboration with the Baloda District Administration, will include tennis, volleyball and badminton courts, a Yoga Bhavan, athletics facilities, and professional coaching designed to prepare rural youth for careers in the Defence Forces, CRPF, and State Police Services.

Inspired by the spiritual and cultural legacy of Shaheed Veer Narayan Singh, Mata Kumkalini, and Dhameshesari Mata, the initiative seeks to expand educational opportunities while promoting dignity, self-confidence, and socio-economic empowerment among disadvantaged communities.

Bhaisa Region Model School (Raipur)

The Bhaisa Region Model School will provide free, quality education from Bal Vatika to Class 12 for children belonging predominantly to the Satnami community and other Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe communities.

Inspired by the ideals of Guru Ghasidas, the school will integrate academic excellence with career counselling, vocational skills, sports, value-based education, and community development programmes, contributing to the long-term social, educational, cultural, and economic upliftment of the region.
